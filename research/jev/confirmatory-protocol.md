# Jev calibration: protocol for the next evaluation

**Status: frozen analysis rules draft; acquisition/model identity lock required before confirmation.** All data, predictions, plots, and outcomes inspected for notebooks 01–04 and this revision are exploratory. This document fixes candidate analysis rules; it is not evidence that a new study has been registered or run. Before collecting predictions, complete and hash the acquisition manifest described below. Changes after that lock require a dated amendment and a separately identified exploratory analysis.

## 1. Claims and targets

Keep three estimands separate. For probability recovery, `q` is an executable exact event probability and the main errors are `mean(abs(p-q))` and excess expected Brier `mean((p-q)**2)`. Expected outcome Brier adds the irreducible term `mean(q*(1-q))`; an oracle therefore has zero recovery error but generally nonzero outcome Brier. For real events, `y` is the realized binary outcome and Brier is `mean((p-y)**2)`. Never replace `y` with whether the most likely class was correct.

For answer confidence, freeze the chosen answer `a` before scoring and use correctness `z=1[a=y]` as the target for confidence `c`. In a binary task, a derived confidence is `c=p` when `a=1`, otherwise `c=1-p`. A method's own thresholded answer uses `a=1[p>=0.5]`, including a fixed positive-class tie rule. Label this a derived answer-confidence diagnostic; it is neither separately elicited confidence nor additional independent evidence. If comparing confidence assessors on the same answers, hold the answerer and every answer fixed. General question answering requires a new answer-and-correctness dataset and a separate annotation protocol.

## 2. Acquisition and split lock

The final manifest must name the intended deployment population, event definition, forecast time and horizon, source owner/version, acquisition dates, sampling frame, customer/source identifiers, outcome maturity rule, feature-availability review, model ID/revision, prompts/schema hashes, inference settings, evaluator commit, seeds, budget, and allowed calls per source record. Record all existing access to the data. Exclude every already-inspected pilot record from confirmation, including records viewed in EDA or used for prompt/baseline development. A different random split of the pilot is not untouched data.

Use three new real-data domains corresponding to credit outcome, deposit subscription, and completed shopping session outcome only if comparable new sources can actually be acquired. The old public UCI datasets remain external exploratory benchmarks: public-data contamination and sampling cannot be ruled out. South German Credit has only 1,000 records, including 300 positives, and its pilot test used the entire 200-record test pool. It cannot provide the sample proposed below, and its sampled bad-credit prevalence is not a lending-population prevalence. Larger Bank and Shoppers pools are useful for exploratory diagnostics but are not automatically independent confirmation after inspecting their distributions.

Keep development/training, calibration, and final test disjoint by source entity before making paraphrases or derived questions. Fit baseline feature transforms only on development. Genuine customer/account/session/source IDs take precedence over feature hashes. Preserve separate observations even when their visible features and outcomes match; retain conflicting outcomes for identical features. Collapse a duplicate export only when a stable source observation ID and export provenance establish it is the same observation. Log that decision. A feature hash is a documented leakage/dependence proxy, not proof of a shared customer or an erroneous record.

For temporal domains use development dates strictly before calibration dates, and calibration dates strictly before final-test forecast dates. Apply a gap at least as long as the outcome horizon and any known leakage window. Resolve customer groups spanning boundaries before outcome inspection: reserve them for an explicitly separate returning-customer analysis or assign the entity wholly to an earlier eligible partition; do not move future records backward into training. Keep a later temporal-shift test and an external-domain test separate from the main in-domain test, with no recalibration on either. Preserve all eligible records within each temporal window; select any budgeted sample before predictions and labels are inspected. Chronological row order without exact dates is only a temporal proxy.

For synthetic data, create disjoint development, calibration (if used), and final sets at the generating-template level. New numerical draws from an inspected template alone do not test template generalization. Final templates must introduce unseen story/parameterization combinations and composition rules with independently executable probability calculations. Lock their generator and hidden seed with a custodian before model/prompt selection. Include supplied-probability and parameter-derived tracks, boundary probabilities, rare events, complements, and disjoint exhaustive events. Sample templates first, then scenarios and prompt variants; retain the nesting in the manifest.

## 3. Frozen comparisons and selection

The candidate primary family contains ten comparisons: one synthetic Noul-minus-Choice excess expected Brier comparison on held-out parameter-derived templates, and three real-data comparisons in each of three domains: raw Noul minus raw Choice, regularized Choice minus raw Choice, and regularized Noul minus raw Noul. Negative differences favor the first method. Analyze each domain separately; do not average prevalences into a universal ranking. Use simultaneous two-sided 99.5% intervals (Bonferroni familywise 95% for ten comparisons). If fewer domains can be acquired, lock the revised family and adjustment before any final-test inspection; do not remove an unfavorable domain afterward.

Practical effect thresholds are 0.0025 excess expected Brier for synthetic recovery and 0.01 absolute Brier for real events. Probability MAE is a co-reported synthetic measure, with 0.01 an interpretable practical difference. For real-event superiority, add a log-loss guardrail: its paired deterioration must have an upper uncertainty bound no greater than 0.02 nats per record, with genuine infinite losses disclosed. Enumerate these guardrails and their multiplicity adjustment in the acquisition lock. These are design choices, not discovered benefits or universally acceptable business costs. Validate their relevance with the intended decision owner before the acquisition lock; otherwise restrict conclusions to these benchmark thresholds.

Compare raw, intercept-only `sigmoid(logit(p)+b)`, and regularized intercept-plus-slope `sigmoid(a*logit(p)+b)` arms for each primitive. Clip only for numerical fitting at `1e-6`, not to silently alter raw scoring. The intercept-only arm fixes `a=1` and minimizes unpenalized summed calibration logistic loss. The slope arm minimizes summed calibration logistic loss plus identity-centered ridge `lambda/2 * (b*b + (a-1)**2)`, with candidate default `lambda=1`. Use numerical bounds `b in [-20,20]` and `a in [0,10]`, exporting convergence and boundary-hit flags. Nonnegative slope prevents ranking reversal, while zero slope or endpoint clipping can create ties. Freeze the default penalty directly, or select from `{0.1, 1, 10, 100}` using grouped development validation only; the choice and objective must be recorded before fitting once on the calibration split. This is a future selection option, not a procedure already performed. Never choose an arm, penalty, prompt, bin scheme, or exclusion using final-test scores. Report every frozen arm even if the selected one is worse. The pilot reanalysis uses fixed settings and is not a search for a final-test winner.

Train the constant prevalence, logistic-regression, and CatBoost baselines using the same development source population. Tune their hyperparameters only with grouped development validation, using forward validation for temporal data. Record training-label counts. Score all methods on identical final-test source IDs and the same target definition. Report intersection-of-valid-predictions comparisons plus each method's full denominator and failure reasons. Do not quietly delete cases where one method fails.

## 4. Metrics, uncertainty, and failure handling

Require the offline evaluator tests to pass before the acquisition lock: oracle `p=q` has zero probability MAE and excess expected Brier and expected outcome Brier `q*(1-q)`; a constant population base rate is calibrated with chance ranking; a strictly increasing overconfidence transform preserves ranks while changing probability loss; label-column reversal is correctly aligned or rejected; complements, categorical normalization, probability bounds, missing predictions, failure denominators, and partition isolation behave as declared. Save the test command and evaluator version. A later evaluator defect requires rescoring every frozen arm and documenting the correction; it does not authorize changing the hypothesis after viewing scores.

Report distinct panels for proper scores (Brier and log loss), calibration diagnostics, discrimination (ROC AUC and average precision for observed binary outcomes), and coverage. Use exact endpoint-aware log loss: a probability zero assigned to an observed positive incurs infinite loss. A clipped sensitivity at `1e-12` must be explicitly labeled. Report the number of endpoint failures. ROC AUC is undefined for single-class samples, and a constant predictor's tied-score AUC is 0.5 when both classes occur.

Retain equal-width ECE10 as a secondary continuity measure. Predeclare the full sensitivity grid of equal-width and equal-mass bins at 5, 10, and 20 requested bins. Equal-mass binning never splits identical predictions; collapse duplicate boundaries and report achieved bin count, bounds, records, independent groups, mean prediction, outcome/reference mean, signed gap, and uncertainty. Show empty equal-width bins and sparse bins. Bin count is not a fitted method-selection parameter. Recompute score-based equal-mass boundaries in each bootstrap replicate without using labels to create boundaries. Calibration-error estimates depend on the estimation design and can be biased; bootstrap intervals do not remove this bias. See [Roelofs et al. (2022)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/roelofs22a.html) and [Vaicenavicius et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaicenavicius19a.html).

Operational calibration tolerances for the main population are absolute signed mean error at most 0.02 and equal-width ECE10 at most 0.03. Prespecified major subgroups use ECE10 at most 0.05 only when their sample-size/positive-count gate below is met. The acquisition lock must enumerate a separate family of `K` calibration-tolerance checks (including targets, arms, domains, subgroups, and shift panels); use Bonferroni intervals with per-check alpha `0.05/K`. Until this list is locked, these are candidate decision rules. To support a tolerance claim, the simultaneous confidence region must lie inside it; a point estimate below a tolerance is insufficient. Treat a passed binned diagnostic as agreement at that resolution, not proof of conditional calibration for every individual or subgroup. Publish the sensitivity grid regardless of its conclusions.

Use at least 5,000 paired bootstrap replicates for the locked report, increasing the count to give at least 25 expected draws in each adjusted interval tail; record Monte Carlo error of interval endpoints when feasible. Resample source groups, retaining every method and prompt variant for the same unit. Average prompt variants within a scenario and scenarios within a template before equal-weight template inference; report record-weighted sensitivity separately. Resampling cannot identify a population distribution from a fixed hand-selected grid. A single underlying dice process has no empirical between-process confidence interval.

Publish two distinct recalibration intervals: (1) resample final-test groups with the fitted calibrator fixed, conditional on that fit and the frozen baseline models; (2) independently resample calibration groups, refit with frozen settings, and resample final-test groups, capturing calibration-fit and test-sampling uncertainty. Neither includes baseline retraining, model-version changes, or prompt-selection uncertainty. Report all refit failures and undefined metrics; do not replace failed fits with successful draws without disclosure.

For real temporal data, resample contiguous calendar blocks separately within calibration and test windows. Set block length from development autocorrelation and the outcome horizon, and freeze the resulting calendar length before test access. Also publish half/double-block sensitivity. For current sparse pilot samples, ordered-row blocks are only a rough sensitivity, not recovery of missing calendar intervals or unknown customer dependence. All intervals require enough approximately independent groups/blocks and assume the collected population represents the claim's scope; cluster bootstrap does not eliminate systematic shift.

Before scoring, validate request-to-record identity, model version, label mapping, positive-event orientation, all finite probabilities within `[0,1]`, missing predictions, schema failures, and mutually exclusive Choice normalization. Audit independently elicited Noul complements and exhaustive dice sums separately. Do not renormalize separate binary forecasts and present them as a native multiclass output. Exclude only corrupt/unrecoverable source records, objectively invalid payloads, premature outcomes, or observations outside the locked population; keep an exclusion log and report denominators before and after exclusions. Retain contradictory outcomes and difficult cases. Coverage below 99% or a between-method difference above 1 percentage point blocks an unconditional superiority claim pending prespecified missing-outcome/worst-case sensitivity.

## 5. Sample size and feasibility

Plan with independent groups, not API calls. For a paired mean loss difference, a normal approximation is `n_eff = ((z_(1-alpha/2)+z_power)*s_D/delta)**2`, where `s_D` is the standard deviation of paired differences estimated from development data or conservative simulations. At 80% power, a two-sided 5% test, `s_D=0.10`, and `delta=0.01`, approximately 785 independent records detect an effect against zero. With ten comparisons (`alpha=0.005`), approximately 1,332 are required. To certify an effect greater than the practical threshold 0.01 using an interval entirely below -0.01, these same calculations require a true effect near -0.02: the distance from the null boundary is then 0.01. Power to reject zero is not power to certify a meaningful effect.

Inflate for clustering and attrition: `n_observed ≈ n_eff * [1+(mean_cluster_size-1)*ICC] / valid_fraction`, and assess unequal cluster sizes by simulation. For example mean size 3, ICC 0.2, and 90% usable coverage turn 1,332 into about 2,072 observations. Temporal dependence needs block-based simulation; do not assume this exchangeable-cluster approximation covers drift. The final manifest must state the actual development variance, number of source groups, cluster sizes, design assumptions, and resulting simulation-based power. If the target is infeasible, state the minimum detectable effect and keep the claim inconclusive rather than changing the threshold after seeing test results.

For synthetic excess loss, `s_D=0.05` and `delta=0.0025` would require about 5,326 independent template/scenario units under the ten-comparison adjustment. This illustrative large requirement is deliberately not replaced by 5,326 paraphrases or Monte Carlo repetitions. Use new development templates to estimate the variance and hierarchical design effect; reduce the claim's scope if genuinely distinct templates cannot be supplied.

Calibration precision also constrains size. An independent Bernoulli event rate near 0.5 needs about `1.96**2 * 0.25 / 0.03**2 ≈ 1,068` observations for a 95% interval with 0.03 half-width, even before multiple-bin and group dependence adjustments. Ten bins cannot each have that precision with 1,068 total records. The pilot's 200 tests and 120 calibration labels do not support fine-grained calibration guarantees. Predeclare a minimum of 100 positive and 100 negative calibration records and 200 positive and 200 negative test records per main domain; obtain them by larger representative windows, not outcome-balanced sampling. At 5% prevalence those gates alone imply about 2,000 calibration and 4,000 test records on average, with a safety margin determined before collection. If any gate fails, report the sparse-event result descriptively and mark the corresponding calibration claim inconclusive. Do not keep extending collection after inspecting effect estimates; extend only under a locked outcome-count rule with a maximum window/sample size.

## 6. Decision table and reporting lock

| Claim | Supports the claim | Contradicts the claim | Inconclusive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noul meaningfully improves probability recovery on new parameter-derived templates | Simultaneous interval for Noul minus Choice excess expected Brier lies below -0.0025; probability MAE also reported | Interval lies above -0.0025, ruling out the specified improvement; interval above zero is evidence of harm | Interval crosses -0.0025; too few independent templates; only familiar templates acquired |
| Noul improves real-event probability quality in a named domain | Paired Brier interval lies below -0.01 on matched records, coverage gate passes, log-loss deterioration upper bound is at most 0.02 nats | Interval lies above -0.01, ruling out the specified benefit; interval above zero indicates harm | Interval crosses -0.01, too few events/groups, log-loss guardrail unresolved, or failure sensitivity can reverse the conclusion |
| Regularized recalibration improves a primitive in a named domain | Calibration-refit/test-resampling Brier interval lies below -0.01 and log-loss guardrail passes; fixed-fit interval reported alongside it | Refit interval lies above -0.01; above zero indicates worsening | Interval crosses -0.01, fits unstable, log-loss guardrail unresolved, or calibration sample cannot satisfy count gates |
| Event probabilities or derived answer confidence meet stated calibration tolerances | Upper uncertainty bound for ECE10 is below 0.03 and signed-error interval wholly inside [-0.02,0.02], with adequate counts and all bin sensitivities disclosed | Lower ECE10 bound exceeds 0.03 or signed-error interval wholly beyond a tolerance boundary | Bounds cross a tolerance; sparse bins/events; alternate bins reveal unresolved local failures |
| Calibration transfers to later time or another domain without refitting | Each separately held-out shift panel meets the same locked quality/calibration criteria | A shift panel rules out the locked threshold or shows a material degradation | Shift data unavailable, too small, or only the original pilot was reused |

Here “contradicts” for improvement claims means contradicts the specified effect size, not necessarily proves the methods differ in the opposite direction. A lower Brier score does not by itself demonstrate calibration. A low ECE does not demonstrate discrimination. Treat temporal/domain results as separate claims; passing the in-domain panel cannot rescue a failed shift panel. Any unplanned comparison is explicitly exploratory.

Archive the protocol and acquisition manifest hashes before inference; preserve requested IDs, successful IDs, failed IDs, source grouping, partitions, and raw probabilities. Record independent source records, source groups, synthetic event definitions, generating templates, prompt variants, and repeated calls separately. The 500 Monte Carlo repetitions of 2,000 outcomes in the old pilot were conditional outcome simulations with frozen predictions; they add zero independent model evaluations. Release the full locked comparison table, including unfavorable and undefined results, without selecting the best prompt, bin count, or correction after test access.
