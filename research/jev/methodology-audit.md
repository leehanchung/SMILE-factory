## Methodological issues and revised scope

1. **Target semantics.** The old UCI Brier calculation already used the observed event `y`, and the synthetic calculation used `q*(1-q)+(p-q)**2`; these formulas were not winner-correctness scoring bugs. The report nevertheless combined distinct targets under a broad calibration heading and lacked a separate answer-confidence diagnostic. The revision names probability recovery, event forecasting, and confidence-versus-correctness explicitly. For derived binary confidence, complement the event probability when the chosen answer is negative. Its Brier and log loss are then algebraically equal to the event scores, although its reliability bins differ; this is another diagnostic view, not independent evidence. An event probability must never be directly scored against chosen-answer correctness.
2. **Population filtering.** The historical rule excluded ambiguous-looking outcomes and removed repeated features before splitting. It cannot distinguish duplicate exports from repeated observations. The population audit retains records by default, reports exact-source-row and feature-proxy repetitions separately, and checks proxy overlap across splits. A complete all-population Jev comparison requires new cached predictions and, ideally, real source IDs. No weight or bootstrap can recover the missing responses.
3. **Uncertainty and evidence counting.** The old real-data bootstrap conditioned on its fitted calibrator and assumed record independence. It did not estimate uncertainty from fitting on only 120 calibration labels. Conditional Monte Carlo ranges quantify outcome simulation given fixed synthetic inputs, not uncertainty over model behavior or new templates. Revised paired analyses must retain related prompts/questions together and label fixed-calibrator and calibration-refit/test-resampling intervals separately.
4. **Metric interpretation.** Brier/log loss measure overall probability quality; ECE is a binned diagnostic; AUC/average precision measure discrimination; coverage measures how often scores exist. They are not substitutes. Credit's historical constant predictor has exact test ECE10 zero because both its training rate and this test prevalence equal 0.30; it still has no rank discrimination. ECE depends on sample size, binning, ties, and the prediction distribution. Resampling its estimator does not eliminate finite-sample or coarsening bias. See [Roelofs et al. (2022)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/roelofs22a.html) and [Vaicenavicius et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaicenavicius19a.html).
5. **Correction and selection.** Original correction used an intercept and slope fitted on reserved calibration labels; legacy fit artifacts remain preserved. The revision adds an intercept-only comparator and identity-centered regularization with fixed settings. Selecting the best arm from these already-seen test results would be exploratory. A fitted correction may improve in-sample reliability while destroying useful variation or failing under prevalence shift. Keep matched Constant, Logistic regression, and CatBoost predictions; their historical fits remain conditional, not newly trained confirmation.
6. **Precision and generalization.** Tests contain only 200 records per dataset and calibration as few as 14 positives. Sparse bins, small group counts, and wide intervals can leave differences unresolved. South German Credit has only 1,000 records and its full original 200-record test pool was used. Unused records from a public, inspected dataset and reshuffled pilot rows cannot be presented as a new independent validation study. New time periods, domains, source entities, and synthetic templates are required.

## Implemented checks versus assumptions

The implemented evaluator is [evaluation.py](src/jev_research/evaluation.py), with 33 evaluator tests passing in `tests/test_evaluation.py` and Ruff checks passing at revision time. Tests cover oracle/base-rate/overconfidence controls; distinct event and confidence targets; label alignment, normalization, complements, bounds, failure coverage, and exact infinite endpoint loss; width/mass 5/10/20 bins preserving ties; paired/group/block sampling; a single scenario yielding no population interval; and fixed-calibrator versus independent calibration-refit/test resampling. Refit diagnostics retain and flag one-class samples, convergence failures, and numerical boundary fits.

[population.py](src/jev_research/population.py) audits source records and grouping, while [revised_analysis.py](src/jev_research/revised_analysis.py) matches cached requests/records, preserves matched baselines, produces paired comparisons and uncertainty, and exports the revised tables. The notebook's generated tables and manifest are the authority for its numerical results and actual uncertainty settings. Verification completed: `make check` passed Ruff lint/format, all 62 tests, and the offline environment/training/calibration smoke check. All ten updated notebook code cells executed without errors. Historical raw Brier and ECE10 reproduce exactly; 1,076 source-run files and the archived original notebook passed SHA-256 preservation checks. `runs/revised-exploratory-v1/verification.json` records these checks and runtime versions. None turns the already-inspected results into confirmation.

Remaining assumptions are distinct from these implementation checks: the retained historical labels are valid; row IDs represent source observations; feature proxies capture some dependence but not all shared customers; local blocks approximate some temporal dependence; sampled source records represent only their stated historical population; and calibration/test resampling approximates repeat sampling within those populations. Label provenance and public benchmark contamination cannot be settled by an evaluator test. Neither group resampling nor regularization removes population shift.

Work requiring new data includes an all-population Jev sensitivity with previously removed records restored, entity-aware splits, an adequately sized calibration sample, genuinely prospective event forecasts, untouched temporal/domain panels, separately elicited answer-confidence tasks if that claim is desired, and held-out synthetic templates. No paid inference is needed for this revision and no such inference is evidence for the present report.
